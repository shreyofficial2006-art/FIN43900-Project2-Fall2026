Good evening. I'm Shrey Goyal, portfolio analyst for the Maple Education Fund. I used Anthropic's Claude to build this tool and draft my materials; the analysis and recommendation are mine. This is education, not investment advice.
Here is the app running live, as of the 30 September review. The headline: pilot, objective not met. The process puts 75% in BIL, $750,000, and 25% in VT, $250,000. The cash estimate is the 2-year Treasury yield as published by then: 4.22% for August.
Every baseline breaks a rule. Equal weight and inverse volatility miss the return objective. The 60/40 benchmark has no reserve and could have lost 33% in a year. The process passes every hard rule but misses 6.5% by 0.46 points.
Watch one control. At a minus 20% loss limit, 6.5% becomes reachable with 65/35. Back at minus 15%, it isn't. That is the decision in front of you.
My recommendation: pilot, do not adopt. Allocate 75% BIL and 25% VT, with VT between 20 and 25%, for the 2026-27 year. From my Lab 20 paper pilot of 55/45, that moves $200,000 from VT to BIL.
Why. The best return inside your minus 15% limit is 6.04%. Each extra point of loss you tolerate buys only about 0.15 points of return. Counting the whole 2008 crisis, world stocks fell 47% in 12 months, and 65/35 would have lost 18.5%, about $185,000.
Has the process earned adoption? No. Deciding every September since 2013 with only what was known then, 6.5% was reachable in 1 of 13 reviews. It earned 4.13% a year against 4.19% for equal weight, and in 2022 it lost 16.4% when stocks and bonds fell together.
The allocation itself is robust. Cutting the stock or cash estimates, raising costs to 75 basis points, or raising the reserve to 20% leaves it at 75/25; only the expected return moves. The open assumption is bond returns, which rest on negative trailing averages. A bond-heavy mix fails our all-worst-at-once stress, so bonds stay out until that is redesigned. The risk of holding 75% in bills is that rates fall.
What we monitor quarterly. If GS2 reaches 4.83%, 75/25 meets 6.5%; October's reading is already 4.74%. If GS2 falls below 3.5%, we review. If the fund loses more than 10% in 12 months we review; past 15% we stop. If bonds re-enter, they must pass the stress first. If a data check fails, we don't rebalance.
My ask. One: approve 75/25 for one year. Two: by March, choose between keeping the minus 15% limit with a 5.9% objective, which I recommend because the limit protects the awards, or keeping 6.5% and accepting minus 20%. Three: fund the redesign of bond estimates. We would adopt the process next September only if the estimation windows agree within 20 points and no trigger fired.
Thank you. I'm happy to take questions.
